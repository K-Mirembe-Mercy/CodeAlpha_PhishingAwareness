Don’t take the bait

Phishing is a scam in which attackers pretend to be someone you trust, such as a bank, an employer or a friend, to steal passwords, money or access. This short, hands-on module teaches you to spot it.

What you will learn

  • Find the red flags in realistic scam emails.
  • Tell real web addresses from fake ones.
  • Recognise the tricks used in social engineering.
  • Know what to do before, and after, you click.
  • Test yourself with a 10-question scored quiz.

All brands, addresses and people in the simulations are fictional. The real cases are summaries of publicly reported incidents. Nothing here collects or sends your data. Your progress stays in your own browser.

Fake websites

Read a web address like a pro

https://login.pearlbank.co.ug/signin

  • https:// means the connection is encrypted. It does not mean the site is trustworthy.
  • Subdomain: the owner of the domain can create any of these.
  • Domain: this shows who really owns the site. Read backwards from the first single slash.
  • Path: anything after the domain. It can say anything.

Real or fake? Test yourself

Quick tips for spotting fake websites

  • Check the whole domain. Attackers put the brand name first, as in brand.com.evil.xyz.
  • Watch for look-alikes. Digits for letters (1 and l, 0 and o), extra hyphens, swapped letters.
  • The padlock is not a trust badge. Many phishing sites use HTTPS.
  • Do not sign in from links in messages. Type the address, use a bookmark or open the official app.
  • Preview shortened links before opening them, or avoid them completely.
  • Be wary of pages that ask for everything at once: password, PIN, card number and OTP.
  • A polished design proves nothing. Copying a logo and layout is easy.
  • Take browser warnings seriously. Do not click through them.
  • Use a password manager. It will not autofill your login on the wrong domain.
  • Shopping sites: if the price is too good to be true, search for reviews and check how new the site is.
